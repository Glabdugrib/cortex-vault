> Reference document for portfolio, blog posts, and LinkedIn content.

## 1. Brand Concept

### The Core Metaphor: The Lab

Everything Simone creates lives inside **the lab** — a space for curiosity-driven experimentation where real engineering craft meets a playful, try-it-and-see spirit. The lab isn't sterile or academic. It's messy benches, blinking prototypes, and half-finished sketches next to polished, working builds. It's where ideas get stress-tested, broken apart, and rebuilt better.

The lab metaphor works because it captures the duality of Simone's brand: the rigor of someone who builds production systems for thousands of users, and the restless curiosity of someone who can't stop tinkering after hours.

### Brand in One Line

**A full-stack engineer who builds serious things without taking himself too seriously.**

### Brand Promise

When you work with Simone or read his content, you get someone who:

- Makes complex technical work feel approachable and human.
- Owns the full picture — product thinking, not just code.
- Treats every project like an experiment worth learning from.
- Shows his process honestly, including the wrong turns.

## 3. Voice & Tone

### Voice (Constant)

Simone's voice is **warm, direct, and casually sharp**. He writes like he talks — conversational, clear, occasionally funny, never trying to impress. He explains things like a smart friend at a whiteboard, not a conference speaker at a podium.

**Voice traits:**

- **Conversational over formal.** First person. Short sentences mixed with longer ones. Contractions. No stiffness.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Show the thing, then explain the principle. Lead with examples, code, or stories — not theory.
- **Honest over polished.** If something broke, say it broke. If a decision was a gamble, call it a gamble. Vulnerability builds trust.
- **Witty, not performative.** Humor shows up naturally — in asides, self-deprecation, unexpected analogies. Never forced punchlines.

### Tone (Shifts by Context)

|Context|Tone Dial|Example|
|---|---|---|
|**Blog post**|Playful, exploratory, "here's what happened in the lab"|_"I spent a weekend trying to make this card animation feel right. Here's the third approach that actually worked."_|
|**LinkedIn post**|Warm but purposeful, insight-forward with personality|_"Most codebases don't rot because of bad developers. They rot because nobody sets patterns early. Here's what I do on day one."_|
|**Project showcase**|Proud but understated, let the work speak|_"A booking app for a local board game club. Simple problem, clean stack, happy users."_|
|**Portfolio copy**|Inviting, energetic, lab-host energy|_"Welcome to my lab. This is where I document my creative process, discoveries, and occasional misadventures."_|
|**CTA / outreach**|Collaborative, low-pressure, warm|_"Got a spark of an idea? My lab is always open."_|

### Signature Phrases & Patterns

These are recurring linguistic fingerprints that make Simone's content feel like _him_:

- **Lab language:** "experiment," "tinkering," "discovered," "built," "broke," "rebuilt," "prototype," "iteration."
- **Process honesty:** "here's what actually happened," "the part I got wrong," "attempt #3."
- **Casual confidence:** stating what he did clearly, without hedging or overselling.
- **Inviting closers:** ending posts with a question or open door, not a lecture.

### Words to Use

experiment, build, tinker, ship, iterate, discover, break, pattern, craft, prototype, explore, dig into, wire up, spin up, hands-on, from scratch, end-to-end, under the hood

### Words to Avoid

leverage, utilize, synergy, revolutionize, cutting-edge, passionate (overused), guru, ninja, rockstar, thought leader, deep dive (overused — prefer "dig into"), robust (prefer "solid"), utilize (prefer "use")

## 4. Writing Principles

### 1. Start with the story, not the stack

Open with _what happened_ or _why it matters_, not a list of technologies. The tech stack earns its mention once the reader cares about the problem.

> ❌ "I built a Vue.js + TypeScript + AWS Lambda booking application using the Serverless Framework."
> 
> ✅ "A local board game club needed a way to let people reserve tables online. I built it in a weekend."

### 2. Show the mess, then the clarity

The most interesting part of building something is the path from confusion to solution. Don't skip the struggle — it's what makes the outcome satisfying and the content relatable.

> ❌ "I implemented a dynamic theming system for the platform."
> 
> ✅ "The client wanted every pharmacy to feel like _their_ app, with their own colors and logo. The first approach was a mess of CSS overrides. The second one actually scaled."

### 3. Write for the founder, not the compiler

Simone's audience includes non-technical people who make hiring and project decisions. Every post should be understandable to someone who doesn't code, even when it goes deep technically. Use analogies. Explain _why_ before _how_.

### 4. One idea per post

Don't try to cover everything. A single insight, well-told, beats a survey of five. If there's more to say, that's the next post.

### 5. End with an open door

Close with a question, an invitation, or a forward-looking thought. Never end with a summary that feels like a school essay conclusion.

> ❌ "In conclusion, this project taught me the importance of clean architecture."
> 
> ✅ "I'm still not sure this is the best approach — if you've solved this differently, I'd love to hear how."

## 5. Content Formats & Templates

### Blog Post Structure

```
HOOK          → A short, specific opening that sets up the problem or curiosity.
CONTEXT       → Why this matters. What was the situation.
THE BUILD     → What happened. Show the process, including wrong turns.
THE TAKEAWAY  → What was learned. Keep it honest and specific.
OPEN DOOR     → A question, invitation, or "what's next."
```

### LinkedIn Post Structure

```
HOOK LINE     → One punchy sentence or observation. Pattern-interrupt.
                (Skip the "I'm excited to announce" energy.)
BODY          → 3-6 short paragraphs. One idea. Concrete example or story.
CLOSER        → Question for engagement OR a warm, opinionated takeaway.
```

### Project Showcase Structure

```
ONE-LINER     → What it is in plain language.
THE WHY       → What problem it solves and for whom.
THE HOW       → Stack and approach, briefly.
THE RESULT    → What it looks like, what happened.
LINKS         → Code, demo, or write-up.
```

## 7. Brand Personality Do's & Don'ts

### Do

- Write like you're explaining something to a smart friend over coffee.
- Share what went wrong alongside what went right.
- Use humor when it comes naturally — never force it.
- Make technical concepts accessible without dumbing them down.
- Show enthusiasm for the craft without being performative about it.
- Use "I built" and "I shipped" — own your work clearly.

### Don't

- Write like a LinkedIn influencer farming engagement.
- Use jargon to sound smart when simpler words work.
- Hedge everything with "I think maybe possibly."
- Write in passive voice to sound "professional." Be direct.
- Oversell. Let the work and the story do the convincing.
- Pretend every project was a smooth, linear success.

## 9. Quick-Reference Checklist

Before publishing any piece of content, check:

- [ ] Would a non-technical founder understand the main point?
- [ ] Does it sound like _me_ — warm, direct, a little playful?
- [ ] Is there a real story, example, or specific detail (not just abstract claims)?
- [ ] Did I show process or honesty, not just a polished result?
- [ ] Does it end with an open door, not a dead end?
- [ ] Would I enjoy reading this if someone else wrote it?
